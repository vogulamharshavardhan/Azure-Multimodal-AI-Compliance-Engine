## Material connections and endorsements

FTC guidance explains that an endorsement is an advertising message consumers are likely to believe reflects someone else's opinions or beliefs rather than the sponsoring advertiser's. The FTC Endorsement Guides apply to social media and other media. An endorsement must reflect the endorser's honest opinion and cannot convey a claim the marketer could not legally make.

A connection between an endorser and marketer that consumers would not reasonably expect, and that could affect how consumers evaluate the endorsement, should be disclosed clearly and conspicuously. Material connections can include payment, free or discounted products, perks, employment, family, personal relationships, or other incentives. The analysis depends on context. A creator who independently discusses a product without a connection to the marketer is not automatically making an endorsement on behalf of that marketer.

Disclosures should be easy to notice and understand, placed with the endorsement, and expressed in clear language. For video endorsements, FTC staff guidance says a disclosure should appear in the video rather than only in the description. Audio and visual disclosure can help reach viewers who watch with or without sound. Platform disclosure tools may not, by themselves, be adequate in every context.

The FTC's guidance repeatedly emphasizes that outcomes depend on the facts, audience understanding, and context. This snapshot must not be used to turn uncertain facts into a definitive violation. When the commercial relationship or endorsement context is unknown, preserve uncertainty and require human review.

## Legal status and limits

The FTC describes the Endorsement Guides as guidance about how Section 5 of the FTC Act may apply. The Guides themselves do not have the force of law, and the FTC states that the facts of a particular case determine whether a practice is deceptive or otherwise violates the FTC Act. This application must report evidence-based findings and must not represent its output as a legal determination.

The FTC states that its Endorsement Guides were revised in 2023. The linked FAQ recommends consulting the latest version of the Guides and related official FTC resources. This snapshot is not exhaustive and must be reviewed by a qualified compliance owner before production use.
